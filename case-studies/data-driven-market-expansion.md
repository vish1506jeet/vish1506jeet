# Data-Driven Market Expansion — Rajasthan (West County of India)

**Company:** Think & Learn Pvt. Ltd.
**My role:** Business Analyst — built the data foundation that senior leadership actually used to decide where the expansion budget went

## The problem in plain terms:

Think & Learn wanted to expand into western India, specifically Rajasthan, and the focus wasn't just the obvious big cities — it was tier 2 and tier 3 towns, which is a much harder call to make well. The marketing team wanted to run lead generation and school-outreach campaigns across the state, but leadership had no real basis for deciding which towns and cities to actually put money behind first.

"Cover everything" isn't a strategy when the budget is limited, it's just a way to spread that budget thin enough that it doesn't really work anywhere. Someone had to turn "we want to expand into Rajasthan" into an actual ranked list of where to go first, and why.

## What I did

This wasn't a report written after the fact to explain a decision that had already been made. It was the analysis that shaped the decision before a single rupee of the budget was committed.

- **Pulled together everything relevant to the region.** School density and count by town, population and demographic data, where existing leads in the state were already coming from, and our own historical conversion data for the areas we already had some presence in. You can't prioritise anything sensibly without this in one place first.
- **Segmented every target town and city by tier and by real opportunity**, not just by size. I scored each one on how many prospective students were actually there, how competitive the market already looked, and how similar towns had converted for us in the past. A big town with a saturated market isn't automatically a better bet than a smaller one nobody else was targeting yet.
- **Went through the existing lead funnel town by town.** This is where the more interesting findings came out — some towns were converting far better than others on the exact same marketing spend, and the data showed roughly why, which fed straight back into the recommendation.
- **Turned all of that into a ranked shortlist**, with the reasoning behind each town spelled out, instead of a gut-feel list of "these seem like good places to try."
- **Took it directly to senior leadership myself.** This is the part that made the difference — it wasn't a report that sat alongside the decision, it's what leadership actually used to decide where the expansion budget and marketing channels got allocated.
- **Kept tracking performance once the prioritised markets went live**, checking it against what the data had predicted, and that fed straight into how the next phase of the expansion got planned. The analysis didn't stop being useful the day the budget was approved.

## What it actually achieved

- A 17% improvement in conversion across the tracked areas
- A 19% uplift in engagement in the prioritised markets, compared to what a blanket rollout across the state would have produced
- A repeatable way of prioritising future expansion markets, rather than a one-off exercise that only applied to Rajasthan

## Why this one mattered to me

Out of everything I've worked on, this is the clearest example I have of data actually changing a real business decision before the money went out the door, not just explaining a decision after it had already been made. Leadership walked into that conversation with a list of towns picked mostly on instinct and experience, and walked out with one backed by actual numbers on where the opportunity really was — and that's the version they went with. For a business analyst, that's the whole point of the job: not producing a report, but changing what a decision-maker actually decides.
